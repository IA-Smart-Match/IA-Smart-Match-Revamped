# Smart Match class exercise: write-up for Oct 28

## 1. How matching works

Each team picks a list of 60 students for each of two events, and CBACH sends the personalized email to the 60 students on that list: Northline (technology) in round one, Harbor (International Business and Marketing) in round two. Everyone also gets the usual mass email, and the 300-student mass email is the comparison.

The team picks a setting with four weights (default 3/3/2/2): major, interest, past events and goal. The weights rank students; the top 60 form the list.

Each student then goes through the same chances:

1. Notices the email: 20 in 100 for the mass email, 60 in 100 for the CBACH email.
2. Signs up, if they noticed: starts at 5 in 100, plus 35 when one of their true interests is a topic of the event, 15 for a fitting career goal, 5 (1 to 2 past events) or 10 (3 or more), and 5 for the targeted major.
3. Attends: 65 in 100. The 8 students already signed up attend at 65 in 100 too.

There is no extra luck: the team's fixed chance draw decides, so the same list gives the same answer twice. The list's value is the "lift": sign-ups the personal email added over the mass email alone. It is a lift, not a guarantee.

At the start of round two (not the end of round one), the team asks all students without a card to fill one in (promise, small reward, or required). Better information makes the same setting choose better in round two.

## 4. What worked and what did not

Worked:
- With 60 emails the lessons are visible. With 30 the list fills quickly and asking barely changes anything.
- Interest first goes from weakest in round one (5.3, few students have a card) to strongest after asking (up to 13.7).
- A stronger way of asking collected more profile cards: 69, 127, 184.

Did not work:
- Major first stays flat at 6.5 to 6.9 whatever is asked. A setting that ignores what students tell us cannot benefit.
- "Required" costs students: 35 stopped responding, so the list loses them.
- A list is only as good as what we know. Round-one Northline invitees were mostly the wrong students for Harbor.
